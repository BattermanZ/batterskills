# The grader brief

The template for the frozen brief in step 2. Fill every `<placeholder>` from the setup answers, keep every other line as it is, and save the result in the log.

The brief carries nothing from earlier rounds: no earlier scores, no findings, no list of what was fixed, no opinion of yours. A grader that knows what was fixed looks for the fix, and one that knows the last score anchors on it.

---

You are grading <the thing> for <who it is for, and how they will meet it: on a phone, once, in their own language>.

**How to reach it:** <URLs, commands, or files, and how to run it if it needs running>.

**Score these categories**, each from 1 to 10 with one decimal (7.4, not 7):

<one line per category: its name, and what it means for this audience>

**The scale.** <With reference examples: "The owner rates these: <example> is a <score>, <example> is a <score>. Score relative to them." Without: "Use your own judgement of what a 9 means for this audience."> Score the thing as it is now, on its own merits.

**Not graded:** <what the grader skips, such as content still to be written>.

**Testing safely:** <side effects to avoid and where to test instead, such as "the preview sends real email, so send the form only on the local copy at <address>, which keeps mail in a folder">.

**Where you work.** Judge from what the thing shows and serves. Save your notes and screenshots in `<round folder>` and leave everything else as you found it. Keep out of the git history and commit messages, and out of `hill-climbing/` beyond your own folder, since those hold earlier rounds. If you need help, ask for it in your report instead of starting another agent.

**Report**, for each category:

- The score, and the reasons for it.
- The evidence: a screenshot path, a measurement, or the steps you took.
- The fixes that would raise it most, best first. When something is broken, name the cause as exactly as you can: the page, the element, the file and line if you can see it.

End with what you could not check, and why.
